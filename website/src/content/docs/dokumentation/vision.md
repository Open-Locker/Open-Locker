## Access should be simple and affordable

Open Locker gives people access to physical resources that would otherwise be
out of reach because of cost, distance, or missing infrastructure. The project
started with a particular focus on young people, socially disadvantaged people,
and rural communities, but it is not limited to specific groups or use cases.

Open Locker was founded as open source infrastructure under the premise of
“Public Money, Public Code.” This origin does not prescribe how the project
will be funded in the future. An optional managed service should sustainably
fund the open core while remaining more affordable than typical market
offerings.

## Our principles

- **Affordability over feature breadth:** Hardware, operation, and use should
  remain as affordable as possible.
- **Trust as the foundation:** Security, privacy, reliability, and traceability
  take priority.
- **Simple to use:** Access should be understandable for people of all ages and
  should not depend on owning a smartphone in the long term.
- **Openness and data sovereignty:** Self-hosting, European data protection,
  open interfaces, and freedom of hardware prevent lock-in.
- **Retrofit instead of replace:** Existing non-smart cabinets should be
  affordable to upgrade.
- **One stable core, many applications:** Optional modules and integrations
  should enable rental lockers, short-term use, pickup, direct sales, item
  lending, and further use cases.

## How we evaluate contributions

A contribution moves Open Locker in the right direction when it expands or
simplifies access to resources, keeps total costs low, and protects the
foundations of security, privacy, reliability, and traceability. It should
strengthen the open core without making every operator pay for individual
special cases.

Open Locker should not become a full ERP, inventory, or lending system, a
surveillance tool, or a closed ecosystem. It does not compete in a costly race
for enterprise feature breadth.

Success means independent organizations use and operate Open Locker over the
long term, retrofit existing cabinets without vendor lock-in, and realize
projects for which commercial alternatives would be too expensive. An active
community and a sustainable managed service support the continued development
of the open core.
